# Ghost environment lighting and FLUDD poses (2.3.3 feedback 4)

The old ghost renderer submitted models into whichever player draw buffers were
current. It never selected the stage's sun/shadow environment for the ghost's
position. Its opacity callback also replaced every native material colour with
white. Keeping a BMD's lighting-channel bits alone could not fix those two
problems.

`lightSetFor` now queries the ghost's ground band and retail shadow cubes and
submits the body and attachments through the already registered object lighting
set. That set installs the level's light positions, colours and ambient colour
when drawing. The choice uses the ghost's position rather than invisible/live
Mario. Invalid floor light indices are bounded before the retail function (whose
original comparison admits equality). A missing/unregistered set retains the
existing draw route; no light group or heap allocation is created after stage
setup. The normal draw-buffer selection is restored after entry.

Body packets retain their material RGB and replace only alpha. The native
`SMS_InitPacket_MatColor` callback cannot do this merely by receiving GX_ALPHA0:
retail PPC code masks the channel to one bit and emits a complete XF RGBA value.
The new callback points at private material colour storage and needs no separate
per-material allocation. Attachments retain their existing callback behavior.

Mario additionally needs its native lighting material pipeline. The original
five-stage shader combines clean/polluted texture selection with a lighting
lookup and a specular stage. The former one-stage texture-times-diffuse ghost
conversion dropped those last two stages, leaving a dark cap/body band despite
correct light binding. Its replacement uses a TVB4 block with three active stages:
clean base texture, unchanged native lighting lookup, and unchanged native
specular combination. Both RGB channels, original texgens, and TEV/konst colours
remain. Alpha passes ghost opacity independently. The old unused TVB16 objects
stay in the same private heap; no shared material or user recolour is changed.

The existing V6 records contain nozzle, emission, aim and timeline observations,
but no separate upper-body animation/frame or nozzle transition controller.
Ghosts now reconstruct a deterministic pumping pose using the retail upper-body
BCK selected by `gMarioAnimeData`, scoped to `chn_chest`, and evaluate private
FLUDD body/nozzle joints from the already loaded retail animation table. Legs
retain their recorded body animation. Frame selection uses the recording's QF,
so a pause holds the pose and a seek does not depend on a free-running animator.
The original BCK frame and root calculator are restored immediately after model
calculation. Yoshi and old recordings without FLUDD observations retain the old
body path. This is a visual reconstruction, not exact recording of the original
limb, pump or switch animation; it cannot recover unrecorded cooldown/empty-tank
pumping.

No ghost format, timeline, input or timer code changes. One small second matrix
calculator per model fits inside the existing model heap; the material callback
change removes the old per-material helper allocations. No memory reservation
or savestate pool changes.

Validation:

- Executable host tests cover sun/ground/cube-shadow choice, airborne clearance,
  invalid light IDs, missing lighting sets, frozen/seekable pump phase and exact
  RGB preservation across all ghost opacity choices.
- Existing Mario-material and model-lifetime checks are included.
- Private US Dolphin rendering uses one body inside 73,728 bytes of the existing
  MEM1 attachment area and the remaining 57,344 bytes for FLUDD, solely to avoid
  the standalone Dolphin fake-MEM2 GPU limitation. Its diagnostic Mario preflight
  is 71,680 bytes. The measured native Mario allocation was 71,064 bytes plus 304
  bytes of heap overhead. Production retains its conservative separate 90,112-byte
  model heaps and full 128 KiB attachment heap; these fixture overrides are not
  production changes.
- The final private fixture (US patched-image CRC32 `1D444A9F`, before its separate
  PAD test double) rendered Mario and the complete FLUDD body/nozzle in Bianco 1.
  A naturally recorded V6 spray was stepped at QFs 624, 628 and 632: body, tank and
  nozzle matrices changed at each step. The stage object-light set was registered
  and active. The FLUDD body used 33,436 bytes and the nozzle 7,476 bytes. The
  separated-player screenshot distinguishes the live Mario from the spraying
  ghost. The owned emulator was stopped after capture.
- This does not validate console GPU addressing, every appearance/nozzle, or two
  simultaneous body models. The earlier 90,112/40,960-byte diagnostic split could
  not admit the FLUDD body's conservative preflight; the final split above did.
  Evidence and precise build scope are under
  `build/v2.3.3-feedback4/ghost-render`, including `runtime/render-proof.json`,
  `runtime/final-pump-1.png`, `runtime/separated-rig.png`, `fixture/scope.json` and
  `fixture/verification.json`.

Follow-up controlled shading check:

- The earlier FLUDD screenshot demonstrated rendering/animation, but still showed
  the one-stage shader's dark band. It was insufficient evidence of correct model
  shading. The following comparison supersedes that visual conclusion.
- A separate US fixture `D499B9F9` copies all 29 live Mario joint matrices into the
  private ghost, recalculates envelopes, and independently admits live/ghost draw
  entry. Position, pose and camera therefore match. Attachments are disabled in
  this fixture so its sole MEM1 body heap can remain the production 90,112 bytes.
- `shading-runtime/same-pose-proof.json` records identical joint matrices. The
  screenshots `live-same-pose.png`, `ghost-three-stage.png` and
  `ghost-one-stage.png` compare the native body, corrected pipeline, and the old
  diffuse formula at the same pose. The old formula is applied only to private
  materials for that diagnostic capture and then restored.
- Sampled coloured interiors give mean absolute RGB error against the native
  image of 2.45 vs 24.43 for the cap, 0.00 vs 31.72 for the shirt, and 3.58 vs
  13.77 for the overalls (three-stage vs one-stage). These are scoped pixel
  comparisons, not a claim of pixel-identical geometry/occlusion: live Mario has
  separate cap/hand/FLUDD rendering, and the ghost uses the embedded body parts.
  Raw regions, masks and results are recorded in
  `shading-runtime/shading-interior-comparison.json`.
- The three-stage Mario uses 83,164 bytes plus 304 bytes of heap overhead, inside
  the unchanged 90,112-byte model heap and existing 87,040-byte preflight. The
  conversion adds 52 initialized code/data bytes over the prior conversion.
- All 20 focused material, lighting/animation and model-lifetime tests pass.
  The corrected shader was checked visually for Mario in Bianco 1 on Dolphin;
  this is not complete Piantissimo/Shadow Mario, every stage/nozzle, two-body, or
  Wii/Wii U hardware verification. No later production change was made after
  this fixture. Both owned emulators were stopped after capture.

The follow-up evidence is under the same `build/v2.3.3-feedback4/ghost-render`
directory, in `shading-fixture/` and `shading-runtime/`. Production source changes
are `src/ghost_model.cpp` (shared lighting/animation) and
`src/ghost_mario_model.cpp` (private Mario shader); fixture pose copying and draw
switching are diagnostic-only.
